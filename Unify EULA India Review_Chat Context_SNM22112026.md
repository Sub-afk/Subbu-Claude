## 2. What Evinova and Unify are

**Evinova**
- Evinova AG, a company incorporated in Switzerland (registered number 205.308.908, Neuhofstrasse 34, 6340 Baar). Operates evinova.com. Listed as seller or developer of the Unify Trials app on the Indian App Store and Google Play.
- A standalone health technology company launched by AstraZeneca (stated in the user's prompt; the Play Store package is com.astrazeneca.unify.gen1). This link is not recorded as a fact in section 2 of the memo, although the HCP gift analysis (U-08, checklist row 34) depends on it.
- Sells digital clinical trial technology to sponsors, CROs and sites. It is not the sponsor and not the investigator.

**Unified Trial Solution (UTS) and Unify**
- One platform reached through an application or through an internet portal (the Web Portal).
- Website description (screenshots): "a digital clinical trial management tool"; "one clinical trial platform, every stakeholder"; single sign-on for patients and sites with modular features configured per trial.

**Features confirmed from the memo and the screenshots**
- Connected devices and telehealth ("capture 74% of assessments remotely ... real-time health insights").
- Novel digital endpoints (remote measurements and patient reported outcomes).
- ePRO and eCOA integration, with pre-configured or custom questionnaires, automated calculations and compliance reporting.
- Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM): "smart algorithms guide patients to recognize and report symptoms early"; "alerts help investigators intervene faster"; "real-time alerts enable sites to intervene quickly"; patients "feel supported knowing their health is monitored at home". Toxicity modules: pulmonary, gastrointestinal, stomatitis and oral thrush.
- Patient app screen shows: "Contact your doctor. Your symptoms are worsening. Please speak to your study doctor or nurse right away so they can advise you."
- Home supply and medication management: direct-to-patient delivery and reminders.
- Multimodal data capture across smartphones, tablets and computers (so patients may also use the portal).
- Multilingual support: 115+ language locales across 50+ countries (the brief says 80 languages; the App Store lists English and 65 others, including Hindi, Bengali, Kannada, Oriya, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu).
- For sites: autonomous workflows (data changes, archiving, study closeout), Study Document Assistant (self-service query resolution), single sign-on, real-time dashboards, automated sample tracking (barcode scanning, linked analytes, biosample management), configurable alerts, integrated telehealth (schedule virtual visits).
- For sponsors: monitor site operations and patient data in real time; performance tracking with role-based access.
- Separately (outside UTS end use): AI Platform Solutions with AI agents for protocol authoring, planning and modelling; consulting services including regulatory consulting and AI-powered patient recruitment.
- App listing: device data capture "where use of devices have regulatory clearance", stated as the US and EU; version notes mention pulse oximetry.

**How it works for each user (one loop)**
- The patient generates data at home (questionnaires, symptoms, device readings), receives reminders, alerts and supplies, and joins telehealth visits.
- Unify carries the data to the site.
- The site (investigator and team) reviews data and alerts, runs visits and acts. Sponsor and CRO staff monitor in real time.

**EULA definitions supplied by the user**
- **HCP**: a healthcare professional involved in administering a clinical study at a clinical study site, instructed to use the Web Portal by the CRO administering the Study or by the Study Sponsor.
- **Other Users** of the Web Portal: employees or agents of the CRO or the Study Sponsor.
- **Patient**: a person participating in the Study who has been referred to use UTS by the Investigator and who has signed the Informed Consent Form (ICF), or has had an ICF executed lawfully on their behalf.

**Meaning of "site"**: a clinical trial site, the hospital, clinic or institution where the trial is run and participants are enrolled, and in practice its team (principal investigator, sub-investigators, coordinators, nurses, pharmacists).

Effect of the user's definitions:
- The HCP EULA is accepted by each individual professional, so duties binding individual doctors (IMC Regulations reg. 6.8, reg. 7.14, Contract Act s. 27) apply to the person accepting.

## 5. How to read the obligations against a finished EULA

Very few laws require specific words in the EULA. Most obligations limit clauses the EULA already has. Three categories:
- **Must be in the EULA**: essentially the Intermediary Rules items (prohibited content, suspension notice, Grievance Officer) and, if anything is charged, the E-Commerce disclosures.
- **Must not be in the EULA**: checks on existing clauses (termination, assignment, indemnity, caps, forum, variation, waivers, time limits, claims, disclosure, content licence, account closure, ICF conflict, capacity, data deletion, arbitration, non-compete).
- **Required by law but not EULA text**: acceptance screen design, ethics committee review of the Patient EULA, language availability, grievance timelines, eConsent signature capture, RBI authorisation of the payment provider, device licensing and authorised agent, CRO registration.

Statements such as "the ICF prevails" or "Unify is not an emergency service" are ways to comply, not words the law prescribes. Given the website claims about real-time alerts and home monitoring, the emergency service statement is now effectively needed.

## 8. Medical device status and S.O. 648(E)

**Relevance of S.O. 648(E).** The Drugs and Cosmetics Act regulates "drugs". Section 3(b)(iv) lets the Central Government notify devices as drugs. S.O. 648(E) of February 11, 2020 (in force April 1, 2020) notified all devices, "including a software or an accessory", intended for diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of disease. It is the gateway: software intended for monitoring becomes a "drug" in law, and the Act's drug provisions apply through the Medical Devices Rules, 2017. The only remaining question is intended use.

**Position after the screenshots.** The global "not a device" position holds for trial management, scheduling, reminders, eConsent, supply logistics and telehealth video. It is likely not to hold for RPM: the app tells the patient their symptoms are worsening and to contact the doctor, algorithms guide symptom recognition, sites get alerts to intervene, and the modules track named toxicities. eCOA becomes a device if automated calculations produce a clinical score used to diagnose, monitor or treat. Connected device integration is at risk (CDSCO guidance Example 5) and may be an accessory.

**Provisions that follow**
- Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940: s. 3(b)(iv) (device as drug); s. 10 (prohibition of import of non-compliant drugs; relevant because Evinova AG is overseas); s. 18(c) (no sale or distribution without licence); ss. 13 and 27 (penalties; amended by the Jan Vishwas Act, 2026 from June 30, 2027); ss. 17, 17A, 17B† (misbranded, adulterated, spurious).
- Medical Devices Rules, 2017: rule 3(v) (intended use is what the manufacturer states in labelling, instructions or promotional material, so the EULA and website count); rule 4 and First Schedule (Class A to D; memo places RPM at Class B to C); rule 3(f) (Indian authorised agent holding the import licence); rule 38(1)(ii) and (x) (licence conditions incl. complaints and adverse events); rule 43A (suspension or cancellation); rule 44 (labelling); rules 40 and 41 (test licence for import for clinical investigation); rules 49 to 54 (clinical investigation, trigger only).
- Related notifications in the sources folder: G.S.R. 102(E), February 11, 2020 (registration of newly notified devices); G.S.R. 777(E), October 14, 2022 (non-sterile, non-measuring Class A exemption, unlikely to help RPM). Phased licensing dates by class are not stated in the memo and were not cited.

**What reaches the EULAs**: intended use statement; identification of manufacturer, authorised agent, licence number and complaint and adverse event route; no disclaimer of fitness for the intended purpose (Patient EULA points 25 to 27; HCP EULA point 16). Everything else is regulatory and sits outside the EULAs.
